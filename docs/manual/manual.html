1. What DAW 388 is

The TASCAM 388 Studio 8 (1985) put an eight-track tape recorder and an 8:8:2 mixing console in one cabinet. DAW 388 does the same on your computer: eight mono tracks on a virtual tape, a console with eight channel strips, eight buses and a stereo master, a plate reverb and a tape echo, and the sound of the tape when it plays back.

It is meant to be simple. There are no plug-ins, no endless tracks and no piano roll: you record, you mix, you export. The keys and knobs carry the names of the 388 (TRK, PGM, AUX, REC, RTZ, CUE), so the manual of the real machine helps too. Where DAW 388 differs from the machine, this manual says so.

The console works in two modes:

  • SIMPLE: one strip per track, as in most recording programs. Choose an input, press REC, play. This is where every new project starts.
  • 388: the full routing of the machine, with the program buses, ASSIGN, DIRECT and the MONITOR section. For ping-pong recording and for working the way the 388 does.

Switching from SIMPLE to 388 does not change the sound. You can learn the program in SIMPLE and switch when you want more.

2. Installing

macOS

macOS 11 or later, on Apple Silicon and Intel.

  1. Open the DMG and drag DAW 388 onto Applications.
  2. Start DAW 388. The app is not signed with an Apple Developer ID, so macOS says it could not verify it. Click Done, not "Move to Trash".
  3. Open System Settings, Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway next to DAW 388. Confirm with your password.
  4. From then on DAW 388 opens normally.

Comfortable with Terminal? This command does the same and skips the warning:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/DAW 388.app"

The first time you choose an input, macOS asks whether DAW 388 may use the microphone. Allow it, or nothing will be recorded. You can change it later in System Settings, Privacy & Security, Microphone.

Windows

Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit. Unzip the ZIP somewhere you like (for example in your Documents) and start DAW 388.exe. If SmartScreen warns about an unknown publisher, choose "More info", then "Run anyway".

DAW 388 uses WASAPI, the standard audio system of Windows. ASIO is not supported yet, so the delay while recording can be a little longer than in programs that use ASIO.

3. The first start

DAW 388 opens with an empty project of eight tracks, in SIMPLE mode, and plays through the default audio device of your computer. Before you record, open Settings (Cmd+,, on Windows Ctrl+,, or File, Settings...) and check three things:

  1. Output: where you listen, for example your audio interface or headphones.
  2. Input: the device you record from. As long as this is "None", nothing can be recorded and no microphone permission is asked.
  3. Buffer: smaller is less delay, larger is safer for a slow computer. 128 or 256 samples is a good start.

These are settings of the app, not of a project: they stay the same whichever song you open. All settings are described in chapter 17.

If the device you chose is not connected, DAW 388 plays through the default device and says so at the top right. As soon as your device returns, it switches back by itself.

4. The screen

The menu bar

At the top: the menus File, Edit, View and Help, the name of the project in the middle (with "(modified)" when there are unsaved changes), and on the right the status line, the time of the last save and the full-screen button. The status line is where DAW 388 tells you what happened or what is missing. Read it when something does not do what you expect.

MIXER and TRACKS

There are two views. MIXER shows the whole console: the meter bridge, eight channel strips, the master section and the tape deck. TRACKS shows the tracks on a timeline for editing, with the meter bridge, the counter and the transport keys above it. Press Tab or click the leather armrest at the bottom of the window to switch.

The size of the console

The console always fits in the window: make the window larger and everything grows, smaller and everything shrinks. Full screen (the button at the top right, or File, Full screen) gives the most room.

On a low screen, such as a laptop with 1920 by 1080 pixels, the full console becomes small to read. Choose View, Compact console: the strips then leave out EQ and the AUX and EFF sends, and the master section leaves out the effects and their masters. Everything that is hidden keeps working, you just do not see it. If you have never made a choice, DAW 388 starts with the compact console by itself when the screen is too low for the full one.

Knobs, faders and keys

  • Drag a knob or fader up or down. Hold Shift for fine adjustment.
  • Double-click a knob or fader to set it back: faders to 0 dB, PAN to the centre, sends to off.
  • Click a key to switch it. A lamp beside or above the key shows whether it is on.
  • Hover the mouse over anything to see what it does and its exact value.

5. Your first recording

A guitar or a voice on track 1, in SIMPLE mode.

  1. Save the project with File, Save (Cmd+S). Recordings are stored in the project, so DAW 388 asks for a name and a place first. If you forget, it asks when you press REC.
  2. On channel 1, choose the input in the red menu at the top of the strip, for example in 1.
  3. Set the INPUT switch to MIC for a microphone or LINE for a keyboard, a guitar through a pedal or another line signal.
  4. Press REC on the channel. Its lamp blinks: the track is ready to record. You now hear the input.
  5. Play and turn TRIM until the OL lamp only flashes on the loudest notes. The TRK 1 meter above the strip shows the level.
  6. Press RTZ (Home) to go to the start, then REC in the transport (R). The tape starts and records. Press STOP (Space) when you are done.
  7. Press REC on the channel again to disarm it, RTZ, and PLAY (Space) to listen.

Not happy? Cmd+Z takes the recording away again. For the next part, arm another track and record over what you hear.

Want a click? Press CLICK (K) and set the tempo under the transport keys. A count-in is set in Settings, Metronome.

6. Projects

A project is one song. On a Mac it is a package Song name.daw388 that the Finder shows as a single document; on Windows it is a folder with that name. Inside are the recordings (in audio), the settings of the song and the last ten backups.

  • File, New (Cmd+N): an empty project with eight tracks in SIMPLE mode.
  • File, Open... (Cmd+O) or Open Recent: open a project. Double-clicking a project in the Finder does not open it yet; use the menu.
  • File, Save (Cmd+S) and Save as... (Shift+Cmd+S). Audio you loaded from elsewhere is copied into the project when you save, so the project is complete on its own. The originals stay where they were.
  • File, Close project (Cmd+W) and Quit DAW 388 (Cmd+Q) ask first when there are unsaved changes.

Once a project has been saved, DAW 388 saves it by itself 30 seconds after every change. A backup is made at every save you do yourself and at most every five minutes otherwise.

Everything that belongs to the song is saved with it: the mix, the tape settings, the metronome, the view, the zero and CUE points and the export folder. Your audio device and the other Settings belong to the app.

7. The channel strip

Each of the eight strips belongs to the track with the same number. From top to bottom:

AUX and PREThe send to the AUX bus and the plate reverb. With PRE on, the send comes before EQ, fader and MUTE (for a headphone mix); off, after the fader.
EFFThe send to the EFF bus and the tape echo, after the fader.

FaderThe level of the channel, from off to +6 dB. The value is shown underneath.

The meter bridge

Above each strip is the TRK meter of its track; on the right the two large meters of the stereo mix. A red lamp in a meter (PEAK) lights near full scale. In SIMPLE mode the TRK meters show the channel, before the fader (PRE) or after it (POST), chosen in the master section. In 388 mode they show what the track's MONITOR row hears, as on the machine: the bus going to the track while recording, otherwise the tape.

8. The master section

To the right of the strips. In SIMPLE mode it is one column; in 388 mode a second column holds the MONITOR section and the PGM masters.

9. Reverb and echo

As on the 388, the AUX bus feeds a reverb and the EFF bus an echo. Turn up AUX or EFF on a channel to send it there. The returns come back into the stereo mix through EFFECT RTN: RTN 1 is the plate (GAIN and a balance PAN), RTN 2 the echo (GAIN and PAN). Effects are never recorded on a track.

PLATE (AUX)

  • DECAY: how long the reverb lasts, 0.5 to 6 seconds.
  • PRE: a short wait before the reverb starts, 0 to 100 ms. It keeps the voice clear of its own reverb.
  • TONE: from dark to bright.

ECHO (EFF)

  • TIME: 40 to 800 ms with FREE, or with 1/4, 1/8 or 1/8. (dotted) in time with the metronome tempo.
  • FEEDB.: how many repeats.
  • TONE: how dark the repeats get. Like a tape echo, every repeat is a little darker and wobbles a little.

Use AUX for a headphone mix (with PRE)? Then turn RTN 1 down, or the reverb comes along. You can also use your own effect: send AUX or EFF to an output of your interface (Settings) and bring it back on a channel set to LINE.

11. Recording in detail

Arming and starting

REC on a channel strip arms its track (REC FUNCTION): the lamp blinks. REC in the transport starts recording on all armed tracks, from stop together with PLAY. Arm several tracks to record several inputs at once.

Without armed tracks, transport REC makes the machine record-ready (REC blinks); pressing REC on a strip then starts recording on that track, and pressing it again punches out.

Punching in and out

To repair a part: play the song, and press REC at the spot where the new take should start (punch-in). Stop recording with PLAY (keeps playing), STOP, or any wind key. With INSERT on you hear the tape until the punch-in, and then your input.

With PUNCH (AUTO PUNCH) DAW 388 punches in and out by itself: set the zero point (RESET) before the part and the CUE point after it, start a little earlier, and press REC. Only the part between the two points is recorded. With REPEAT on, you can try again and again; the last take stays.

Count-in

With a count-in (Settings, Metronome) REC from stop first counts one or two bars while the tape waits, then starts on the beat.

Recording on VARI

As on tape, you can record with PITCH on VARI, for example a fast part slowed down. While recording the speed is fixed. Played back at normal speed, what you played at +10% sounds slower and lower.

Where the recordings go

Every take becomes a 24-bit WAV file in the project's audio folder and a region on its track. A new take replaces what was under it, like on tape, but the old recording is not lost: Cmd+Z brings it back, and in the TRACKS view you can drag its edges out again.

Delay (latency)

Your computer and interface need a moment to get the sound in. DAW 388 measures this and places every recording that much earlier, so it lines up with the tape. If your recordings still sit slightly late or early, adjust Latency in Settings by a few milliseconds.

Hearing yourself

Normally you hear your input through DAW 388, with EQ and effects. If your interface has direct monitoring and you prefer that (no delay at all), switch off Monitoring in Settings; DAW 388 then plays nothing of the input.

12. The 388 routing

Switch ROUTING to 388. There are now two separate paths, as on the machine: what goes onto the tape, and what you hear.

What goes onto the tape

  • Channel source: MIC, LINE or RMX. RMX (remix) is the playback of the track with the same number, for mixing and ping-pong.
  • ASSIGN sends the channel to a pair of program buses: 1-2, 3-4, 5-6 or 7-8. PAN divides it between the odd (left) and even (right) bus of the pair. The PGM MASTERS ODD and EVEN set the level of buses 1, 3, 5, 7 and 2, 4, 6, 8.
  • Track N records bus N, or with DIRECT channel N straight away, after TRIM and EQ and before the fader. DIRECT is not on the real 388: it is a quick way to record one input on one track.
  • AUTO (also not on the 388): the channel plays the tape, and switches to the input while its track records. This is how SIMPLE mode works.

What you hear

  • L-R on a channel sends it to the stereo mix, with its fader, PAN, MUTE and EQ.
  • The MONITOR section has a row per track with its own GAIN, PAN and mute (M), and a TRK meter. With MON on PGM each row hears its bus; on CUE an armed track hears its input and the other tracks hear the tape.
  • The STEREO MASTER gets the channels on L-R. Only when no channel at all is on L-R does it get the MONITOR mix instead. That is the recording setup of the 388: channels to the buses, listening through MONITOR.

Recording a track and hearing nothing? Then its channel is not on L-R while other channels are. DAW 388 warns about this when you press REC on the strip. Either switch L-R on for that channel, or switch it off on all channels and listen through the MONITOR section.

Recipes

  • One input on one track (guitar on 3): channel 3 on LINE or MIC with its input, DIRECT on. Arm track 3 and record. Set how loud you hear it in MONITOR row 3.
  • A stereo source (on 5 and 6): channel 5 on the left input, channel 6 on the right, both DIRECT. In MONITOR, pan 5 left and 6 right.
  • Ping-pong to mono (tracks 1 to 3 onto 4): channels 1 to 3 on RMX, ASSIGN 3-4 on, PAN fully right (bus 4). The faders set the balance. Arm track 4 and record.
  • Ping-pong to stereo (tracks 3 to 5 onto 7 and 8): channels 3 to 5 on RMX, ASSIGN 7-8 on; PAN is now the place between bus 7 (left) and 8 (right). Arm both tracks. In MONITOR pan 7 left and 8 right, and switch M on for 3 to 5 if you do not want to hear them twice.
  • Mixdown: all channels on RMX with L-R on. The faders and PAN are the mix.

Ping-pong records the tape as it plays back, so it takes the tape character with it, as on a real machine.

13. The TRACKS view

Press Tab for the tracks on a timeline. Each track has a small fader and PAN knob (the same as in the mixer) and its regions: pieces of recordings or loaded files.

Moving around

  • Click in the time bar at the top to put the tape there; drag in it to select a time range over all tracks.
  • Scroll to move through time. Cmd and scroll zooms around the mouse; Cmd+Left and Cmd+Right or the − and + keys zoom out and in; FIT shows the whole song.
  • While playing, the view pages along with the tape.

Editing regions

  • Click a region to select it; Shift-click adds or removes; click an empty spot to deselect.
  • Drag to move the selection, also to another track. Alt and drag moves a copy.
  • Drag the edge of a region to shorten it, or to bring back what was cut off, up to the end of the recording.
  • What lands somewhere replaces what was there, like on tape.
  • SNAP makes regions snap to the beats, to other regions, the zero and CUE points and the play position. Hold Cmd while dragging to move freely.

The toolbar

KeyWhat it does
SPLIT (S)Splits the selected regions at the play position; without a selection, all tracks.
CUT (Cmd+X)Cuts the selection. A time range is a splice: what comes after it moves up.
COPY (Cmd+C)Copies the selected regions or the time range.
PASTE (Cmd+V)Pastes at the play position, each region on its own track. A time range is inserted.
DELETE (Delete)Removes the selection or the time range, without moving anything up.

Drop audio files from the Finder or Explorer onto the window: they go on the track you clicked last (and the ones after it), from the play position. "File, Load 8 audio files..." puts up to eight files on tracks 1 to 8 from the start.

Undo (Cmd+Z) and redo (Shift+Cmd+Z) go back up to ten steps for everything that changes the tracks: recordings, loading, clearing and editing. Not during a recording.

14. Tape settings

Press TAPE or choose File, Tape settings... The tape character works on playback only: recordings stay clean, so you can change or switch off the sound of the tape at any time. The settings belong to the project.

  • Character: Clean (the tape passes untouched) or 388 (the sound of the machine).
  • Tape speed: 3¾, 7½ (the speed of the 388) or 15 IPS. Slower is warmer, with more wow and less high end.
  • Tape type: 456 style (plenty of headroom), 250 style (warm, less headroom), 911 style (a little brighter).
  • Amounts: Saturation, Wow & flutter, Noise (hiss) and Crosstalk, from 0 to 200%. 100% is the machine as it is.
  • dbx for tracks 1-4 and 5-8: noise reduction. The hiss drops away in silences and breathes along with the music, as on the real machine.

A new project starts on 388, 7½ IPS, 456 with dbx on.

15. Metronome

  • CLICK (K) switches it on and off. TEMPO and METER under the transport keys set the tempo and time signature; TAP sets the tempo by tapping.
  • Bar 1 starts at the beginning of the tape, so moving the zero point does not shift the rhythm.
  • In Settings, Metronome: the volume, a count-in of one or two bars, and whether the click also sounds during normal playback (otherwise only while recording).
  • The click is only heard: it is never recorded and never exported.

16. Export

Press EXPORT or choose File, Export... Save the project first.

What

  • Stereo bounce: the mix as you hear it, optionally with the tape character.
  • Separate tracks: one file per track you tick, with a choice of processing: with mixer (EQ, PAN, fader) and tape character, in stereo with its own reverb and echo; tape character only, mono; or completely dry, mono, the recording itself.

Range

The whole song, or only from the zero point to the CUE point. Every file starts at 0:00, or with silence before it so that it lines up with the song (handy for importing into another program).

Format

WAV or AIFF, 16 bit (with dither), 24 bit or 32 bit float, 44.1, 48 or 96 kHz. The sample rate starts on the rate of the audio in the project.

Name and folder

The files are called Name_mix and Name_01_Trackname. Name is the project name, unless you type another in the Name field. They go into a folder Song name export next to the project; Choose folder... picks another one, which the project remembers. After the export, Show in Finder (on Windows: Show in Explorer) opens the folder.

The export runs in the background and sounds exactly like the app. Reverb and echo ring out at the end.

19. When something does not work

I hear nothing at all

  • Look at the status line at the top right: is the device you chose connected? Check Output in Settings.
  • Are PHONES, the STEREO MASTER and the channel faders up, and is LISTEN on STEREO?
  • Is a track on SOLO? Then you only hear the soloed tracks.

I hear my instrument but nothing is recorded, or I do not hear my input

  • Is an Input device chosen in Settings, and does the channel have the right input (red menu)?
  • On a Mac: does DAW 388 have permission for the microphone (System Settings, Privacy & Security, Microphone)?
  • Is the track armed (REC on the strip blinks)? Is Monitoring on in Settings?
  • In 388 mode: is the channel on L-R while others are? See the tip in chapter 12.

REC does nothing

The project has to be saved first; DAW 388 asks for it. A track that is still loading, or whose file is missing, cannot record until it is loaded or cleared.

Clicks and dropouts

Choose a larger Buffer in Settings, and close other programs that use audio.

Recordings sit slightly late or early

Adjust Latency in Settings by a few milliseconds, and record a test.

Everything is tiny

Use full screen and View, Compact console (chapter 4).

A track says "missing"

A file of the project is not where it should be, for instance after moving files by hand. Put it back, or clear the track. Projects saved with DAW 388 keep their audio inside, so they can be moved and copied whole.

20. Files and folders

  • A project: Song name.daw388 with project.json (the song), audio (the recordings and copied files) and backups (the last ten versions of project.json). On a Mac, right-click it and choose Show Package Contents to look inside.
  • Exports: the folder Song name export next to the project, unless you chose another.
  • The app settings: settings.json in ~/Library/Application Support/DAW 388 (Mac) or %APPDATA%\DAW 388 (Windows). Delete it to start with the default settings.

Audio files that DAW 388 reads: WAV, AIFF, FLAC and MP3, mono or stereo, at any sample rate. MP3 files start and end with a little silence; that is how MP3 works.
